Safer trading

Agree the exchange, protect your account and record what actually happened.

Offer a copy you can actually exchange

Start with your spare-copy count. A blueprint marked Owned means it is in your collection; it does not mean that you hold a physical extra copy to give someone. Confirm the duplicate in your current in-game inventory before offering it.

If you have learned a blueprint and hold one extra copy, record Owned with Dupes Owned set to one. After exchanging that spare, reduce the duplicate count to zero while keeping Owned on. This avoids advertising a copy you no longer have.

Review a listing’s item names and requested exchange carefully. Similar names, an old listing or an out-of-date count can lead to a disagreement even when both players intend a fair exchange.

Make the agreement specific

  1. Confirm the exact blueprint or items each player will provide.
  2. Check quantities and whether the offer is an exchange or a giveaway.
  3. Agree how and when you will coordinate in the game, using the current game rules.
  4. Keep the agreement and relevant listing or offer references so that any later report has context.

A listing that asks for nothing should be understood as a giveaway only when the giver confirms that intention. Do not silently replace an agreed item with a different one or add new requirements after the other player has committed.

If details are unclear, resolve them before entering the exchange. An unanswered question about quantities is a reason to pause, not a reason to assume the most favourable interpretation.

Understand what the hub can and cannot guarantee

UAG helps players coordinate. It is not an escrow service and does not hold in-game items until both sides complete a trade. A message, accepted offer or reputation indicator cannot guarantee that the other player will deliver.

Use reputation as one signal alongside the actual conversation and agreed terms. Be cautious if a player changes the deal repeatedly, pressures you to hurry or asks you to move into an unrelated payment or account-access process.

Check the current rules of ARC Raiders before arranging an exchange. This guide describes coordination through the companion; it does not authorise activity that the game’s rules prohibit.

Protect account access and payment details

Never give another player your password, one-time sign-in code, recovery code or private account credentials. A blueprint exchange should not require anyone to sign in to your account.

Do not send real-money payments as part of a blueprint deal, follow an unexpected “verification” payment link or pay a supposed account-unlock fee. Keep account and billing questions with the official service concerned. UAG support is contact@mobcorp.co.uk.

Before following a link, inspect where it leads. A familiar display name or logo is not enough to establish that a page is genuine. If you have already exposed credentials, use the relevant account provider’s recovery and security controls promptly.

Record the result honestly

Mark an exchange complete only after the agreed items have actually changed hands. Update your collection and spare-copy counts to reflect the result, and close or correct a listing that is no longer available.

If the deal does not complete, retain the conversation and the listing or offer reference. Report the factual sequence through the available reporting route or support. Include what was agreed and what occurred, without exposing passwords, authentication codes or unrelated personal data.

A report helps establish context for moderation; it cannot make the hub recover an item that it never held. Avoid retaliatory claims or publishing another player’s personal information. A clear, specific account of the exchange is more useful than speculation about motives.
